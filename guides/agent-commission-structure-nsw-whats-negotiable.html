How agent commission is usually structured in NSW (and what's negotiable)

How real estate agent commission works in NSW: what the agency agreement must say, common structures, when it's payable and what you can negotiate — general information, not legal or financial advice.

Short answer: In NSW, agent commission is not set by law. It is whatever you and the agent agree in the written agency agreement, which must state the commission and fees, when the agent is entitled to be paid and how payment is made. The amount, the structure and many of the terms are negotiable, so compare written quotes from more than one agent before you sign. This is general information only, not legal or financial advice.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. This guide reflects NSW Government and NSW Fair Trading pages checked on 17 November 2026. Rules can change, so check the current official pages and speak with a solicitor or licensed conveyancer before you sign an agency agreement.

Is real estate commission set by law in NSW?

No. NSW Fair Trading's approved guide for sellers says the amounts charged by agents are not set by law, and you can negotiate with the agent about the amounts of any commissions, fees or other expenses you may be required to pay (Agency agreements for the sale of residential property, FTR32). We don't publish typical rates, because there is no official figure.

What must the agency agreement say about commission?

Before an agent can market your property, you must sign a written agency agreement with them. NSW Fair Trading says the agreement must state (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW):

  • the services the agent will provide;
  • the amounts of any fees or commission you agree to pay for those services;
  • the circumstances in which the agent is entitled to payment (for example, commission is usually payable only when the property is sold);
  • how and when payment is made (for example, whether the agent can deduct commission from the buyer's deposit);
  • warnings about circumstances in which you might have to pay commission to more than one agent; and
  • a warning if the agreement says commission is payable even if the sale is not completed.

For the full pre-signing checklist, read Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first.

What commission structures might you be offered?

NSW guidance refers to commission rates, flat fees and commissions that change based on the sale price. In practice you may see a rate (a percentage of the sale price), a fixed dollar fee, or a tiered arrangement where the rate or fee changes at different sale prices.

NSW Fair Trading urges care with price-based commissions: you may end up paying significantly more than you would have with a flat fee. It suggests researching properties for sale and sold in your area so you have a reasonable estimate of the likely sale price, doing your own calculations, and knowing what you will be liable to pay at the end of the sale (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW). A simple way to do this is to work out the commission at a low, middle and high realistic sale price for each quote. Also ask whether each quoted figure includes GST, so you compare like with like.

When does commission become payable?

Usually only when the property is sold, but the agreement decides. Check the clause that says when the agent is entitled to payment, whether commission can come out of the deposit, and whether there is any term making commission payable even if the sale does not complete (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW).

NSW Fair Trading also tells agents they are not entitled to any commission or expenses unless a written agency agreement was signed by the licence holder and the client, it complies with the regulations, and a signed copy is served on the client within 48 hours after the client signs (Agency agreements – information for property professionals).

Could you end up paying commission to two agents?

Yes, if you are not careful. Under an exclusive agency agreement, the agent may be entitled to commission if the property sells during the fixed term, even if you or another agent find the buyer, and possibly if it later sells to someone who started negotiating with that agent. Under a sole agency agreement, no commission is payable if you find a buyer yourself who was not introduced by the agent. Under an open (general listing) agreement, you pay the agent who finds the buyer (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW).

If you are unhappy with an agent, properly end that agreement before signing with another, or both may charge you commission when the property sells. See Sole agency vs open listing in NSW.

Are marketing and other expenses part of the commission?

Not necessarily. If set out in the agreement, the agent can ask you to pay for things such as advertising, an auctioneer's fee, cleaning, decorating or landscaping. For residential property, the agreement must state the source and estimated amount of any rebates, discounts or commissions the agent will receive on those expenses, and you can negotiate whether you pay the full amount (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW; Agency agreements – information for property professionals). See Marketing costs explained: what you may be asked to pay upfront and Selling costs and timeline in the Hunter.

What can you negotiate?

The amounts of commission, fees and other expenses, and the terms of the agreement. NSW Fair Trading says you have the right to negotiate the terms and conditions and to ask for any legally permitted changes, with alterations signed by all parties (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW). Things worth discussing:

  • the commission rate or fee, and whether it is flat or tiered;
  • which marketing and other expenses you pay, and whether any are payable if the home doesn't sell;
  • the length of any fixed term and how much written notice you need to give to end it;
  • any clause about commission after the agreement ends.

NSW Fair Trading suggests talking to a few agents and asking each for a printed list of their fees, commission rates and expenses. Don't be pressured; re-negotiate or find another agent if you are not satisfied. After signing, you usually have a one-business-day cooling-off period (see Cooling-off periods when selling in NSW).

Have the new underquoting rules for agency agreements started?

NSW Fair Trading said on 17 November 2026 that further changes relating to agency agreements, setting and reviewing estimated selling prices based on comparable properties, prescribed record keeping obligations, and the Statement of Information are paused and will now commence on 1 December 2027. Existing underquoting obligations still apply, including ensuring the estimated selling price is reasonable, revising it when it is no longer reasonable, and not advertising or quoting a price below the estimated selling price in the agency agreement (Changes to property and stock agents laws).

That estimate matters for commission too, because it is the price you will use to work out what each quote would cost you. Read Estimated selling price and underquoting rules in NSW and What to ask at a property appraisal.

Questions to ask each agent about commission

  • Is your commission a rate, a flat fee or tiered? What would I pay at three realistic sale prices?
  • Do the figures include GST?
  • Exactly when is commission payable, and can it come out of the deposit?
  • Is anything payable if the property doesn't sell or the sale doesn't complete?
  • Which marketing and other expenses would I pay, and are any rebates or discounts involved?
  • How long is the fixed term, and how do I end the agreement?

FAQ

Is real estate commission set by law in NSW? No. NSW Fair Trading's approved guide says the amounts charged by agents are not set by law, and you can negotiate commissions, fees and other expenses with the agent.

When do I usually pay commission? NSW Fair Trading says commission is usually payable only when the property is sold, but the agency agreement sets out exactly when the agent is entitled to payment and how it is paid, for example whether it can be deducted from the buyer's deposit. Read that clause before you sign.

Is a price-tiered commission a good idea? It depends on the numbers. NSW Fair Trading warns that when commission changes based on price you may end up paying significantly more than with a flat fee, so work out what you would pay at a few realistic sale prices first.

Can I end up paying two agents? Yes, in some cases. NSW Fair Trading warns that if you sign with a new agent before properly ending your agreement with the first, both agents may charge you commission when the property sells.
