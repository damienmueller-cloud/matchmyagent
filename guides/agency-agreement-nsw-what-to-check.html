Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first

Before you sign with a selling agent in Newcastle or the Hunter, check the estimated price, commission, costs, term and cooling-off rights in the agency agreement.

Short answer: In NSW, an agent needs a signed agency agreement before they can market your home. Before you sign, check the agent's estimated selling price and the evidence behind it, the commission and every fee or expense, when commission becomes payable, how long the agreement runs and how it ends. You also usually get a one-business-day cooling-off period after signing.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. Rules can change, so check the current NSW Fair Trading guidance or ask a solicitor or conveyancer about your situation.

If you're still picking an agent, start with our guides How to choose a selling agent in the Hunter and What to ask at a property appraisal. This guide covers the next step: the paperwork.

What is an agency agreement?

It's the contract between you (the seller) and the agent. It sets out the services the agent will provide, what you'll pay and when. According to NSW Government guidance, the agreement becomes binding once both you and the agent have signed it.

What must the agreement include about price?

The agreement must include the agent's estimated selling price. This can be a single figure or a range, and the top of a range can't be more than 10% above the bottom. The agent must also give you evidence that the estimate is reasonable, such as recent comparable sales. If the estimate stops being reasonable, the agent must tell you in writing and update the agreement.

What to check:

  • Is the estimate backed by real comparable sales you can look at yourself? (See Understanding comparable sales.)
  • If one agent's estimate is far higher than the others', ask why. NSW guidance warns sellers to be cautious of much higher estimates.

What will you pay, and when?

Look for:

  • Commission: the rate or flat fee, whether it includes GST, and exactly when it's payable (usually only on a completed sale, but read the clause).
  • Marketing and other expenses: what's included, what's extra, and whether you pay even if the property doesn't sell.
  • Price-tiered commissions: NSW guidance notes these can mean paying significantly more than you expected, so run the numbers at a few sale prices.

Commission and fees are negotiable. If you're not comfortable, you can renegotiate or talk to another agent.

How long does it run, and how does it end?

Check:

  • The type of agency (for example sole/exclusive or open/general) and what that means if another agent or you find the buyer.
  • The term: the start and end dates.
  • How to end it: notice periods, and whether commission could still be payable after it ends (for example if a buyer introduced during the term buys later).

Is there a cooling-off period?

Usually yes. For residential agency agreements in NSW there's a cooling-off period of one business day. It starts when you sign and ends at 5pm on the next business day or Saturday (public holidays don't count). You can waive it only in limited circumstances: the agent must have given you the proposed agreement (and the approved consumer guide) at least one business day before you sign, and you must sign the approved waiver form.

Quick checklist before you sign

  • Estimated selling price (single figure or a range within 10%) plus written evidence
  • Commission rate/fee, GST, and when it's payable
  • Itemised marketing and other costs, and what's payable if there's no sale
  • Type of agency and the term (dates)
  • How to end it, and any post-term commission clause
  • Cooling-off: confirm you haven't been asked to waive it without the required documents
  • The agent's licence (see How to check a real estate agent's licence in NSW)
  • A copy of everything you signed

FAQ

Do I have to sign an agency agreement to sell with an agent in NSW? Yes. NSW guidance says you must sign one before an agent can market a residential property for you.

Can the agent's price range be wide? No. If a range is used, the highest price can't exceed the lowest by more than 10%.

Can I cancel after signing? Usually, within the cooling-off period (until 5pm on the next business day or Saturday), unless it was validly waived.

Is commission negotiable? Yes. NSW guidance encourages sellers to negotiate commissions, fees and expenses.
