Estimated selling price and underquoting rules in NSW: a seller's guide

NSW underquoting rules for sellers: the agent's estimated selling price, the 10% range rule, the evidence to ask for, ad limits and the changes still to start.

Short answer: In NSW, your agent must write their estimated selling price into the agency agreement, keep it reasonable, back it with evidence, and never quote or advertise a lower price to buyers. Doing so is underquoting, an offence under the Property and Stock Agents Act 2002. Stronger rules, including a Statement of Information and a price in advertisements agents arrange, have been passed but haven't started yet: NSW Fair Trading says they are currently expected towards the end of 2026.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. Rules can change, so check the current NSW Fair Trading guidance or ask a solicitor or conveyancer about your situation.

This guide follows on from What to ask at a property appraisal and Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first. It covers the price side of that paperwork.

What is underquoting in NSW?

An agent underquotes if they make a statement or publish an advertisement about a residential property's price that is less than their reasonable estimate of its likely selling price. NSW Fair Trading says it is an offence under the Act and that it takes a zero tolerance approach.

The laws are there so buyers don't waste time and money on inspections, reports and auctions for homes that are out of their price range.

What is the estimated selling price?

It's the agent's estimate of the likely selling price of your home, and the agent can't enter into an agency agreement to sell your home unless the agreement includes it. It can be a single figure or a price range, but the top of a range can't be more than 10% above the bottom.

NSW Government guidance gives this example: a range of $500,000 to $550,000 is acceptable, but $500,000 to $600,000 is not.

What evidence should the agent give you?

Before or when they set or revise the estimate, the agent must give you evidence that it is reasonable. NSW Government guidance says the estimate should be based on factors such as recent comparable sales, location, property features and current market conditions.

NSW Fair Trading's guidance for agents also lists factors an agency's written procedures should consider, including sales of comparable properties, feedback from potential buyers, any current valuations, the property's features and how it will be marketed. Ask to see the comparable sales and check them yourself (see Understanding comparable sales in the Hunter).

If different agents give you different estimates, each must back theirs with evidence. NSW guidance says to be cautious of much higher estimates, as they may not be reasonable.

What happens if the estimate changes?

If the estimate stops being reasonable, the agent must tell you the revised estimate in writing and amend the agency agreement. Your consent isn't needed for that change.

After revising it, the agent must take all reasonable steps to amend or retract any advertisement showing a price below the new estimate. NSW guidance also says the agent must pass on any offers made, and NSW Fair Trading tells agents to request offers in writing and update the estimate if offers change the likely selling price.

What can't the agent say or advertise?

The agent, and anyone working for them, must not suggest to buyers, in an ad or in conversation, that your home may sell for less than the estimated selling price. Where the estimate is a range, that means anything below the bottom of the range.

  • No "offers over" or "offers above" a price, and no "+" after a price (for example "$500,000+").
  • When the agent tells someone a likely price while marketing your home, they must make a written record of the address, the price or range, and the date and time, and keep it for at least 3 years.
  • If a home is passed in at auction and the last bid was a vendor bid (a bid made by the auctioneer for the seller), the agent can't quote that bid amount unless they clearly say it was a vendor bid.

Can you ask the agent not to show a price?

Under the current rules, yes. NSW Government guidance says you can tell your agent not to disclose the estimated selling price to potential buyers, and the agent then must not give any price information in advertisements, in writing or verbally.

Note that the changes still to start (below) will require agents to include a selling price or price range in advertisements they arrange, with limited exemptions such as a for-sale sign on the property.

What if you want more than the agent's estimate?

NSW Fair Trading's guidance says the agent can advertise at a higher price that meets your instructions. That isn't underquoting, as long as no price below the estimated selling price is advertised or disclosed.

The same guidance warns that quoting the estimate when you've said you'll only sell for more may be false or misleading under the Australian Consumer Law. If you simply disagree with the estimate, NSW guidance notes the law requires the agent to set it using their own professional skills and market knowledge.

What are the penalties for underquoting now?

NSW Fair Trading's current guidance says it can issue a penalty infringement notice of $2,200, and a court can fine an agent up to $22,000. An agent could also lose the commission and fees for an underquoted property, and face disciplinary action on their licence.

What's changing, and when?

The Property and Stock Agents Amendment (Underquoting and Other Agent Conduct) Act 2026 is starting in two stages. NSW Fair Trading says some changes started on 29 June 2026, and the rest "will start on a date to be announced, which is currently expected to be towards the end of 2026." When we checked on 20 October 2026, the current version of the Act on the NSW legislation website still listed those parts of the 2026 amending Act as not commenced.

Started 29 June 2026 (selected): higher maximum court penalties for many offences, and new NSW Fair Trading disciplinary powers, including being able to require a licensee in charge or an independent valuer to verify an agent's estimated selling price.

Still to start, according to NSW Fair Trading:

  • Comparable sales and records: agents will have to consider the sold prices of comparable properties in line with prescribed requirements when setting or revising the estimate, and keep certain records.
  • Statement of Information: agents will prepare one in an approved form, including comparable sales they identified. It must be in (or linked from) online ads, displayed at inspections, and given to buyers within 2 business days if they ask for it or for the contract.
  • A price in ads: agents will need to include a selling price or price range in advertisements they arrange, with limited exemptions such as a for-sale sign on the property.
  • Price floors: an advertised price can't be lower than the estimated selling price, the highest registered bid if the property was passed in at auction, or a written offer you rejected only because it was too low. Online ads must be updated within one business day.
  • Penalties: up to $110,000 or three times the agent's commission (whichever is higher) for underquoting, and up to $27,500 for Statement of Information breaches.

Until those changes start, the rules earlier in this guide are the ones in the current version of the Act. Check the NSW Fair Trading changes page (linked below) for the start date.

What should you check as a seller?

  • The estimated selling price is in the agency agreement (a single figure, or a range within 10%)
  • You have the written evidence for it, and you've looked at the comparable sales yourself
  • You've compared estimates from more than one agent and asked about any that look much higher
  • Draft ads don't use "offers over", "offers above" or a "+" after a price
  • If you don't want a price shown, you've told the agent in writing
  • Any revised estimate comes with written notice and an amended agreement
  • You've asked to be told about every offer
  • The agent's licence is current (see How to check a real estate agent's licence in NSW)

FAQ

Is it underquoting if my home sells for more than the agent's estimate? Not by itself. NSW Fair Trading says a sale price higher than the estimated selling price is not proof of underquoting, because it could be due to strong competition between buyers.

Can the agent's price range be wide? No. If the estimate is a range, the highest price can't exceed the lowest by more than 10%.

Can I ask my agent not to quote a price to buyers? Under the current rules, yes. The agent then must not give any price information in advertisements, in writing or verbally. The NSW changes still to start will require a price or price range in advertisements agents arrange, with limited exemptions.

Have the new NSW underquoting laws started? Partly. Some changes started on 29 June 2026. The rest, including the Statement of Information and prices in advertisements, start on a date to be announced, which NSW Fair Trading says is currently expected to be towards the end of 2026.

Who do I contact if I'm worried about underquoting? NSW Fair Trading, on 13 32 20 (8:30am to 5pm, Monday to Friday).
