Selling a tenanted property in NSW: notice and access basics

NSW landlords: the notice your tenant must get before buyer inspections, limits on access, and how sale-related termination works since the 2025 rental law changes — general information, not legal advice.

Short answer: You can sell a tenanted property in NSW, and the tenancy does not end just because you list. You must give your tenant written notice at least 14 days before the first buyer inspection, keep to the access rules, and — if you want the property empty — use a valid sale ground with the right notice period and supporting documents. This is general information only, not legal or financial advice.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. NSW rental laws changed in 2025 and parts changed again in 2026. This guide reflects NSW Fair Trading pages checked on 10 November 2026. Check the current NSW Fair Trading guidance, and speak with a solicitor or your property manager, before you give any notice or sign anything.

Can you sell a property with a tenant in it in NSW?

Yes. Putting the property on the market does not end the tenancy; it continues unless it is ended lawfully.

NSW Fair Trading says that since 19 May 2025 landlords need a reason to end a tenancy — for periodic leases and at the end of a fixed term — and those changes also apply to leases in place before that date. A property "being sold or offered for sale with vacant possession" is one of the listed reasons (Changes to rental laws). Decide early, with your agent and solicitor, whether you are marketing to buyers who may keep the tenant or selling with vacant possession, because different rules apply.

What notice must the tenant get before buyers inspect?

At least 14 days' written notice before the first inspection by prospective buyers (Minimum notice periods for access to rental property).

Notice periods are counted in calendar days, including weekends and public holidays, and the day the notice is served is not counted. If a notice is posted, NSW Fair Trading says an extra 7 working days must be added.

How many inspections or open homes can be held?

After the first inspection, the tenant can agree on a suitable time frame but is not obliged to agree to more than 2 inspections per week, with 48 hours' notice each time (NSW Fair Trading).

The NSW Fair Trading pages describe showing the property to "prospective buyers"; they do not set separate rules for "open homes". Ask your agent to agree an inspection schedule and format with the tenant in writing before marketing starts.

When can the agent and buyers enter?

In most cases, not on Sundays, public holidays or outside 8am to 8pm — even when correct notice has been given (Landlord access and entry to a rental property).

NSW Fair Trading also says that, where practical, the tenant should be told an approximate day and time; people entering should not stay longer than necessary; and an authorised person such as a selling agent must have written consent from the landlord or agent and show it to the tenant if the tenant is home. If correct procedures are followed and the tenant prevents entry, the tenant may be in breach of the agreement. Tenants can complain to NSW Fair Trading about unlawful entry and contact NCAT if the problem is serious or persistent.

Can you take photos or videos inside for the listing?

Yes, but with notice, a chance for the tenant to move belongings out of shot, and the tenant's written consent before publishing any images that show their belongings.

NSW Fair Trading's access page says photos or videos for advertising can be taken once in the 28 days before marketing starts, with notice and a reasonable opportunity for the tenant to move belongings that can reasonably be moved. Its Changes to rental laws page says changes that commenced on 21 September 2026 require 7 days' notice before photos or video for advertising, and written consent before publication if the tenant's possessions are visible (tenants may withhold consent in domestic-abuse situations). A valuation needs at least 7 days' notice and is limited to once in 12 months.

Can you end the tenancy because you are selling?

Possibly. NSW Fair Trading lists two sale grounds: proposed sale (you are selling with vacant possession and need the property vacant) and actual sale (a contract for sale has been entered into and it requires vacant possession) (Landlord ending a tenancy).

Minimum notice periods listed by NSW Fair Trading (Minimum notice periods for ending a residential tenancy):

  • Proposed sale: 60 days for a fixed-term agreement of 6 months or less; 90 days for a fixed term of more than 6 months or a periodic agreement.
  • Actual sale: 30 days.

NSW Fair Trading says landlords cannot end a fixed-term agreement early except in special cases, and the sale grounds are not among them — so plan around the end of the fixed term. Allow an extra 7 working days if the notice is posted.

What must go with a sale termination notice?

A written, signed and dated notice stating the property address, end date and ground, plus a termination information statement and supporting documents for the sale ground (Giving notice to end a residential tenancy).

  • Proposed sale: either a copy of the proposed contract for sale (showing the details NSW Fair Trading lists, including that vacant possession is required and the required attachments), or a copy or extract of your agency agreement showing the parties, the property, the agent's licence number and the agreement's duration.
  • Actual sale: either a copy or extract of the signed contract (including buyer and seller names, signing date, proposed settlement date and vacant possession), or a written statement from your solicitor or conveyancer confirming those details.

Contact details and other information not required can be redacted. NSW Fair Trading says giving false or misleading supporting documents is an offence and penalties apply; if documents are missing or incomplete, the termination may not be valid.

What happens after a sale termination notice?

If you end a tenancy on the proposed-sale ground, NSW Fair Trading says you cannot rent the property out again for 6 months after the termination date.

A tenant can apply to NCAT to challenge a termination notice if the process was not followed, the supporting documents were false or misleading, or the ground was not genuine. If a tenant does not move out by the termination date, the landlord can apply to NCAT for a termination order and must follow the lawful eviction process (Landlord ending a tenancy).

How does this fit with the rest of your sale?

The usual NSW selling steps still apply: a written agency agreement before an agent markets the property, and a prepared contract for sale before advertising.

Note that a copy of your agency agreement or proposed contract can double as the supporting document for a proposed-sale notice, so the order of steps matters — ask your agent and solicitor to map the timing against your lease dates. When interviewing agents, ask how they will schedule inspections and communicate with your tenant. FAQ

Can I sell my NSW investment property while a tenant lives there? Yes. Selling does not by itself end the tenancy. You must give the tenant written notice at least 14 days before the first buyer inspection, and follow NSW access rules while the property is marketed.

How many buyer inspections does a tenant have to allow? NSW Fair Trading says that after the first inspection the tenant is not obliged to agree to more than 2 inspections per week, with 48 hours' notice each time.

Can I end the lease because I'm selling? Possibly. NSW lists proposed sale and actual sale (where vacant possession is required) as grounds, each with minimum notice periods, a termination information statement and supporting documents. A fixed term generally can't be ended early on these grounds.

How much notice is needed to end a tenancy for a sale? NSW Fair Trading lists 60 days (fixed term of 6 months or less) or 90 days (longer fixed term or periodic) for a proposed sale, and 30 days for an actual sale. Add 7 working days if the notice is posted.
