What a contract for sale needs before your home is advertised in NSW

NSW homes can't be advertised until a contract for sale is ready: who prepares it, the documents it must attach, pool certificates and missing-document risks.

Short answer: In NSW, your home can't be advertised or offered for sale until a contract for sale has been prepared by a solicitor or licensed conveyancer. The contract must attach set documents, including the title search, a drainage diagram, the council zoning (planning) certificate and a statement of the buyer's cooling-off rights, plus a pool certificate if you have a pool or spa.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. Rules can change, so check the current NSW Government and NSW Fair Trading guidance, and have a solicitor or conveyancer prepare your contract.

This guide covers the paperwork you need ready before your agent can start marketing. It sits alongside Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first, which covers your contract with the agent rather than the contract with your buyer.

Do you need a contract for sale before listing your home in NSW?

Yes. NSW Government guidance says you must have a contract of sale prepared by a lawyer or licensed conveyancer before you advertise, whether you sell with an agent or privately.

NSW Fair Trading puts it this way for agents: residential property can't be offered for sale until a contract of sale has been prepared. So if your contract isn't ready, your agent can't start marketing, which makes it worth organising early.

Who prepares the contract for sale?

A solicitor or licensed conveyancer. NSW guidance says they should prepare the contract and will need to represent you at settlement, which in NSW is completed electronically by solicitors or conveyancers who subscribe to the eConveyancing system.

NSW guidance says an agent can handle the marketing and negotiating, but the contract and settlement are handled by a conveyancer or solicitor, and a real estate agent can't change any part of the contract. Your agency agreement must state the extent of the agent's authority, for example whether they can exchange the contract or make changes to it on your behalf, so check that clause (see our agency agreement guide).

What counts as advertising or offering your home for sale?

More than a listing online. NSW Fair Trading says an agent is taken to have indicated a home is for sale if, without a proposed contract available, they do, cause or permit any of the following:

  • advertise or promote the property in a way that indicates it's for sale
  • place a sign on or near the property indicating it's for sale
  • advertise or give notice that the property is to be auctioned in future
  • advertise it at any premises, vehicle or place where the agent does business
  • show or give the property's address to a prospective buyer

NSW Fair Trading also says the draft contract must be available for inspection at the agent's office. Where agents work together under a conjunction agreement, generally only the listing agent needs to hold the contract, as long as the other agents can access it.

What documents must be attached to the contract?

NSW guidance lists the title documents, a drainage diagram, the council zoning certificate and the cooling-off statement. NSW Fair Trading says these documents are required by section 52A of the Conveyancing Act 1919 and prescribed in Schedule 1 of the Conveyancing (Sale of Land) Regulation 2022.

Based on the NSW Government and NSW Fair Trading pages, the contract must include:

  • a copy of the property certificate (title search), the registered plan, and dealings recorded on the title, such as easements, restrictions on use or positive covenants
  • a drainage diagram
  • a current zoning certificate (section 10.7 planning certificate) issued by your local council
  • a notice about smoke alarms and loose-fill asbestos insulation, unless it's printed in the contract
  • a statement of the buyer's cooling-off rights in the prescribed form

NSW Fair Trading says off-the-plan sales need more, including a disclosure statement and a draft plan. Your solicitor or conveyancer will know what applies to your property.

What if your home has a swimming pool or spa?

You need one more document. NSW guidance says one of these must be attached to the contract:

  • a valid certificate of compliance
  • a valid occupation certificate (issued in the past 3 years) and evidence the pool has been registered, or
  • a valid certificate of non-compliance

NSW guidance says this doesn't apply to a lot in a strata or community scheme with more than 2 lots, or to off-the-plan contracts. You can check whether a pool has a valid certificate of compliance on the NSW Swimming Pool Register website.

What else goes into the contract?

The terms of the sale, what's included and excluded, and any issues your conveyancer needs to address. NSW guidance says the conveyancing laws also build certain terms and warranties into the contract, which is why a solicitor or conveyancer should prepare it.

NSW guidance also says that, whatever your selling method, you must disclose any known issues with the property, such as defects or encumbrances. If you own land that may be subject to land tax, NSW guidance says land tax obligations may need to be disclosed in the contract and outstanding land tax must be cleared, so confirm your land tax status with your solicitor or conveyancer before you sell.

What happens if a required document is missing?

The buyer may be able to get out of the sale. NSW guidance says that if a required disclosure document isn't attached, the buyer may be entitled to rescind the contract within 14 days of exchange, unless settlement has already happened.

That's a real risk after you've accepted an offer, so ask your solicitor or conveyancer to confirm every required document is attached before marketing starts.

How does the contract fit with your agent and your price?

They run side by side. Before an agent can advertise or promote your home, you must sign a written agency agreement with them, and that agreement must include the agent's estimated selling price.

Underquoting laws prohibit advertising a property below the agent's estimated selling price (see Estimated selling price and underquoting rules in NSW). Further changes, including a Statement of Information that agents must give buyers who request it or a copy of the contract for sale, will start on a date to be announced, which NSW Fair Trading says is currently expected to be towards the end of 2026.

If you haven't chosen an agent yet, see How to choose a selling agent in the Hunter and the printable agent comparison checklist. For the agreement type, see Sole agency vs open listing in NSW.

What happens to the contract once you find a buyer?

It's signed and exchanged. NSW guidance says neither buyer nor seller is legally bound until signed copies of the contract are exchanged, and if the agent arranges the exchange, they must give copies of the signed contract to each party or their solicitor or conveyancer within 2 business days.

For a private treaty sale, the buyer usually has a 5 business day cooling-off period, which can be waived, reduced or extended by negotiation. There's no cooling-off period for sellers once contracts are exchanged, and none for buyers at auction. NSW guidance says settlement typically happens 6 weeks after exchange, although a different timeframe can be negotiated. For more, see Auction vs private treaty in the Hunter and Selling costs and timeline in the Hunter.

What should you check before your home is advertised?

  • A solicitor or licensed conveyancer is engaged and preparing the contract
  • The title search, registered plan and title dealings are attached
  • The drainage diagram is attached
  • A current council zoning (section 10.7 planning) certificate is attached
  • The smoke alarm and loose-fill asbestos notice is attached or printed in the contract
  • The buyer's cooling-off statement is included
  • If you have a pool or spa: a valid compliance certificate, occupation certificate with registration evidence, or non-compliance certificate is attached
  • Inclusions and exclusions are written into the contract
  • Any known defects or encumbrances have been discussed with your solicitor or conveyancer
  • Your land tax status has been checked, if relevant
  • Your written agency agreement is signed, including the estimated selling price and the agent's authority over the contract
  • The agent has the contract available before any sign or ad goes up, or the address is given to buyers

FAQ

No. NSW Government guidance says you must have a contract of sale prepared by a lawyer or licensed conveyancer before advertising, and NSW Fair Trading says residential property can't be offered for sale until a contract of sale has been prepared.

Who prepares the contract for sale in NSW? A solicitor or licensed conveyancer. NSW guidance says the agent handles marketing and negotiating, but the contract and settlement are handled by a solicitor or conveyancer, and an agent can't change any part of the contract.

What documents must a NSW contract for sale include? NSW Fair Trading also lists a smoke alarm and loose-fill asbestos notice, and a pool certificate is needed if there's a pool or spa.

Do I need a pool certificate to sell my home in NSW?
