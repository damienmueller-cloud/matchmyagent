Marketing costs explained: what you may be asked to pay upfront

How real estate marketing costs work in NSW: what the agency agreement must say about advertising and other expenses, rebates, when you might pay and what to ask — general information, not legal or financial advice.

Short answer: In NSW, an agent can ask you to pay for marketing, such as advertising, on top of commission, but only if it is set out in your written agency agreement. The amounts are not set by law and are negotiable. Whether you pay upfront, during the campaign or at settlement depends on what you agree, so get the costs and the timing in writing before you sign. This is general information only, not legal or financial advice.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. This guide reflects NSW Government and NSW Fair Trading pages checked on 19 November 2026. Rules can change, so check the current official pages and speak with a solicitor or licensed conveyancer before you sign an agency agreement or pay any costs.

Can an agent charge you for marketing on top of commission?

Yes, if it is set out in the agency agreement. NSW Fair Trading says that, if set out in the agreement, the agent can ask you to pay for advertising, an auctioneer's fee, cleaning, decorating or landscaping (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW). Before signing, it suggests talking to a few agents and asking each for a printed list of their fees, commission rates and the expenses (for example, advertising) they charge.

The NSW Government also suggests asking each agent for a full list of fees, including advertising, promotion and administrative costs, and finding out how they will promote your property, for example online listings, open homes, letterbox drops or newspaper ads (Using an agent or selling privately).

Are marketing costs set by law in NSW?

No. NSW Fair Trading's approved guide says the amounts charged by agents are not set by law, and you can negotiate with the agent about the amounts of any commissions, fees or other expenses you may be required to pay (Agency agreements for the sale of residential property, FTR32). We don't publish typical marketing costs, because there is no official figure and campaigns vary.

What must the agency agreement say about marketing costs?

It must state all commissions and any other costs you may be liable to pay. The approved guide says the agreement must specify what the agent is authorised to do for you, such as arranging advertising, and must state all commissions and any other costs you may be liable to pay (Agency agreements for the sale of residential property, FTR32).

For residential property, the agreement must also include a statement identifying the source and estimated amount of all rebates, discounts and commissions the agent will receive in respect of the expenses you pay under the agreement (Agency agreements – information for property professionals). Ask the agent to set out each marketing item and its estimated cost in writing so you can see exactly what you are agreeing to.

For the full pre-signing checklist, read Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first.

Can an agent recover expenses without a proper agreement?

Generally no. NSW Fair Trading tells agents they are not entitled to any commission or expenses unless a written agency agreement has been entered into, signed by the licence holder and the client, complies with the regulations, and a signed copy is served on the client within 48 hours after the client signs (Agency agreements – information for property professionals).

It also says an agent is not entitled to any expenses from the client if the required rebate, discount and commission disclosures are not made. An agent who failed to serve the agreement within 48 hours may, in limited circumstances, apply to a court or tribunal to recover all or part of the commission or expenses (Agency agreements – information for property professionals). If you are asked to pay for something that isn't in your agreement, ask the agent to explain why, and get advice before you pay. Any change to the agreement should be in writing; the approved guide says alterations need to be signed by all parties (Agency agreements for the sale of residential property, FTR32).

What are rebates and discounts on advertising?

They are amounts the agent receives from a provider, which can mean the agent pays less for a service than you are asked to pay. The approved guide gives the example of a provider paying the agency a commission at the end of the year based on how much advertising was placed. The agreement must state the amounts or estimated amounts of any such commissions or discounts and who they come from, and you can negotiate with the agent about whether you should pay the full amount (Agency agreements for the sale of residential property, FTR32; Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW).

Do you pay marketing costs upfront or at settlement?

It depends on your agreement. The official guidance we checked doesn't set a single rule for when marketing is paid, so ask each agent and make sure the timing is written into the agreement.

  • Will I be invoiced before the campaign starts, during it, or will costs be paid from the sale proceeds at settlement?
  • Do I still pay the marketing costs if the property doesn't sell, or if I end the agreement?
  • What happens if the campaign costs more than the estimate in the agreement?

Commission works differently: NSW Fair Trading says the agreement must state when the agent is entitled to payment and how and when payment is made, and that commission is usually payable only when the property is sold (Agency agreements for the sale of property in NSW). See How agent commission is usually structured in NSW. Also note that a residential property can't be advertised for sale until a contract for sale has been prepared (Using an agent or selling privately); see What a contract for sale needs before your home is advertised in NSW.

What if you cancel during the cooling-off period?

Money you have already paid the agent must be refunded in full. For residential property, you have a cooling-off period of 1 day (a business day or Saturday) after signing, and you can cancel by giving the agent written notice. If you do, neither you nor the agent is liable to pay any commission, damages, costs or expenses in connection with the agreement or its rescission (Agency agreements – information for property professionals). See Cooling-off periods when selling in NSW.

Have the new rules for agency agreements started?

No. NSW Fair Trading said on 17 November 2026 that the commencement of changes relating to price advertisements, agency agreements, setting and reviewing estimated selling prices based on comparable properties, prescribed record keeping obligations and the Statement of Information is paused, and these will now commence on 1 December 2027. Existing underquoting obligations still apply (Changes to property and stock agents laws). Read Estimated selling price and underquoting rules in NSW.

Questions to ask each agent about marketing costs

  • What marketing do you recommend for my property, and what is the estimated cost of each item?
  • Which of these costs would I pay, and are any included in your commission or fee?
  • When do I pay: upfront, during the campaign or at settlement?
  • Do I pay if the property doesn't sell or I end the agreement?
  • Will you receive any rebates, discounts or commissions on these costs, and from whom?
  • Do the figures include GST?

FAQ

Do I have to pay for marketing upfront? Not necessarily. NSW guidance doesn't set one rule for when marketing is paid. It depends on what you agree with the agent, so ask whether you pay upfront, during the campaign or at settlement, and make sure the timing is written into the agency agreement.

Are real estate marketing costs set by law in NSW? No. NSW Fair Trading's approved guide says the amounts charged by agents are not set by law, and you can negotiate the amounts of any commissions, fees or other expenses you may be required to pay.

Can an agent charge me for marketing that isn't in the agency agreement? NSW Fair Trading says the agent can ask you to pay for things like advertising if they are set out in the agreement, and an agent isn't entitled to any commission or expenses without a compliant, signed written agreement. If you're asked to pay for something that isn't in your agreement, ask the agent to explain and get advice before you pay.

What is a rebate or discount on advertising? Sometimes the agent pays a provider less than you are charged, for example because the provider gives the agency a commission or discount. For residential property, the agreement must state the source and estimated amount of these, and you can negotiate whether you pay the full amount.
