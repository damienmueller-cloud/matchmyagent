Downsizing in Lake Macquarie and Port Stephens: planning the sale

Planning to downsize in Lake Macquarie or Port Stephens? The NSW selling steps, the ATO downsizer super rules, Age Pension sale-proceeds rules and retirement village contract basics — general information, not financial or legal advice.

Short answer: Downsizing in Lake Macquarie or Port Stephens follows the same NSW selling steps as any home sale. What changes is the planning around it: the order of selling and buying, whether a downsizer super contribution suits you, how the sale money is treated for the Age Pension, and — if you are moving to a retirement village — the village contract timing. Get financial and legal advice before you sign anything. This is general information only, not legal or financial advice.

General information only, not legal, tax or financial advice. This guide reflects ATO, Services Australia, Moneysmart and NSW Government pages checked on 12 November 2026. Rules and amounts can change, so check the current official pages and speak with a licensed financial adviser, a solicitor or conveyancer, and Services Australia before you act.

For local selling context, see Selling in Lake Macquarie and Selling in Port Stephens.

Do the usual NSW selling rules apply when you downsize?

Yes. There is no separate NSW selling process for downsizers. If you use an agent you must sign a written agency agreement, and whatever way you sell, you need a contract of sale prepared by a lawyer or licensed conveyancer before the property is advertised (Selling property in NSW).

The NSW Government also lists disclosing known issues with the property and providing any necessary certificates, such as a valid swimming pool compliance certificate if applicable. Read Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW and What a contract for sale needs before your home is advertised in NSW.

Should you sell first or buy first?

There is no official rule; it depends on your finances and how much risk you can carry. Map the timing with your solicitor or conveyancer and, if you need a loan, your lender before you list.

Questions worth answering early: Where will you live between settlement and moving into your next home? Does your next purchase depend on this sale settling first? If you are moving to a retirement village, NSW Fair Trading says some villages let you sign a village contract and pay the ingoing contribution later, once your home is sold, sometimes with a deposit (Choosing a retirement village). Your agent can talk through settlement-period options with buyers, but they are not a substitute for legal advice. For general process context see Selling costs and timeline in the Hunter.

What costs should you plan for?

Moneysmart lists real estate agent fees, stamp duty, legal fees and moving costs such as furniture removal as costs you may need to pay when you downsize, and notes that a unit or apartment may come with strata or body corporate fees (Downsizing in retirement).

Moneysmart also says how much money you free up depends on the property market, and if you buy and sell in the same market the difference in price may be small. We do not publish prices or market figures. Ask each agent you interview for their fees and marketing costs in writing — see our agent comparison checklist.

Can you put money from the sale into super?

Possibly, through a downsizer contribution. The ATO says that if you are 55 or older you may be able to contribute up to $300,000 from the sale of your home to your super fund, and each eligible spouse can contribute up to $300,000 (ATO: Downsizer super contributions).

The ATO lists conditions you must meet, including:

  • you are 55 or older when you make the contribution;
  • you and/or your spouse owned the home for 10 or more years before the sale;
  • the home is a residential building in Australia (not a caravan, houseboat or mobile home);
  • the sale qualifies, fully or partly, for the main residence capital gains tax exemption (or would have, for a home bought before 20 September 1985);
  • you have not made a downsizer contribution before;
  • you give your fund the Downsizer contribution into super form before or when you contribute; and
  • you contribute within 90 days of receiving the sale proceeds (usually settlement), unless the ATO grants an extension.

The ATO notes that a downsizer contribution doesn't count towards the contribution caps but is included in your total super balance, may affect income support such as the Age Pension, and that you should consider independent financial advice before proceeding.

How does selling your home affect the Age Pension?

Your principal home isn't counted in the assets test while you live in it. Once you sell, Services Australia says the portion of the sale proceeds you plan to use to buy, build, rebuild, repair or renovate a new principal home may be exempt from the assets test (Services Australia: Real estate assets).

For homes sold from 1 January 2023, Services Australia says that exemption lasts up to 24 months, with a possible further 12 months depending on your circumstances. Proceeds set aside for the new home are deemed at the lower rate only; other sale proceeds held in financial assets are deemed at the regular rates. Services Australia also says you must tell them about changes to your real estate, including selling or buying, within 14 days, and that Australian tax residents selling property need a clearance certificate to avoid having 15% withheld from the sale. Moneysmart suggests talking to the Services Australia Financial Information Service before you sell.

Moving to a retirement village: what should you check?

Read the village contract carefully and get independent legal and financial advice before signing. NSW Fair Trading says you must receive a copy of the contract at least 14 days before you sign it (Choosing a retirement village).

NSW Fair Trading also says all residents have a 7-day cooling-off period after signing (it ends immediately if you move in during that time) and a 90-day settling-in period after moving in. Its prospective resident checklist suggests visiting a few villages, understanding entry costs, ongoing charges and departure fees, and asking what happens if you later decide to leave.

How do you choose the right agent for a downsizing sale?

Compare more than one licensed local agent, and ask how they would handle the timing your move needs. Check their licence, ask for their estimated selling price and the evidence behind it, and get fees and marketing costs in writing before you sign.

FAQ

Is there a NSW rule that is just for downsizers selling their home? Not in the selling process itself. The usual NSW steps apply: a written agency agreement if you use an agent, and a contract of sale prepared by a lawyer or licensed conveyancer before the home is advertised.

Can I put money from selling my home into super? Possibly. The ATO says people aged 55 or older may contribute up to $300,000 each from the sale of a home owned for 10 years or more, if they meet all the conditions, give their fund the downsizer form, and contribute within 90 days of receiving the proceeds. Get independent financial advice first.

Will selling my home affect my Age Pension? It can. Services Australia says the part of the sale proceeds you plan to use for a new principal home may be exempt from the assets test for up to 24 months (homes sold from 1 January 2023), with a possible further 12 months. Other money from the sale may count. Ask the Services Australia Financial Information Service before you sell.

What if I am moving into a retirement village? NSW Fair Trading says you must get a copy of the village contract at least 14 days before signing, there is a 7-day cooling-off period after signing, and a 90-day settling-in period after moving in. Some villages let you pay the ingoing contribution once your home is sold. Get independent legal and financial advice before signing.
