Sole agency vs open listing in NSW: what's the difference?

Sole, exclusive and open (general) agency agreements in NSW: who can sell your home, when commission is payable, two-commission risk and cooling-off.

Short answer: In NSW, a sole agency agreement gives one agent the right to sell your home, but you pay no commission if you find a buyer the agent didn't introduce. An open listing (also called a general listing or open agency agreement) lets you list with a number of agents, and you pay commission to the agent who finds the buyer. Under an exclusive agency agreement, the agent may be owed commission even if you or another agent sells during the fixed term.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. Rules can change, so check the current NSW Fair Trading guidance or ask a solicitor or conveyancer about your situation.

This guide builds on Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first. It covers one clause in that paperwork: the type of agency, and what it means for who gets paid.

What's the difference between sole agency and open listing in NSW?

The difference is who can sell your home and who you pay. With a sole agency, one agent has the right to sell, but you can still find a buyer yourself; with an open listing, you can list with a number of agents and you pay commission to the agent who finds the buyer.

NSW Government guidance describes five types of agency agreement for selling residential property: exclusive, sole, general listing or open agency, multiple listing, and auction. The type you sign affects your rights and the commission you may have to pay, so check which one your agreement says it is.

What is an exclusive agency agreement?

It gives one agent exclusive rights to sell your home. NSW guidance says this may entitle the agent to commission if the home sells during the fixed term, even if you or another agent sells it.

The agent may also be entitled to commission if the home later sells to someone who started negotiating with that agent. NSW guidance says exclusive agency agreements are commonly used for selling residential property.

What is a sole agency agreement?

NSW guidance says it is similar to an exclusive agency agreement: you give one agent the right to sell, but you may find a buyer yourself. If you find a buyer the agent didn't introduce, no commission is payable to that agent.

People sometimes use "sole agency" loosely to mean any one-agent agreement. Because NSW guidance treats sole and exclusive agreements differently, read the commission clause to see whether you would still owe commission if you found the buyer yourself.

What is an open listing (general agency) agreement?

It lets you list your home with a number of agents, and you pay commission to the agent who finds the buyer. NSW Government guidance calls this a general listing or open agency agreement.

Each agent needs a written agency agreement signed by you and the agent before they're entitled to commission or expenses, so check each agreement for its commission terms, costs and how it ends. Agreements can also say whether you pay some expenses, such as advertising, so ask each agent what you would pay and when.

What are multiple listing and auction agreements?

With a multiple listing, you deal with an agent who is part of a network of agents working together to sell your home, and you pay commission to the agent you signed up with. An auction agency agreement is an exclusive agency agreement where the home is listed for auction.

If you're weighing up auction against a private sale, see Auction vs private treaty in the Hunter.

Could you end up paying two commissions?

Yes, it can happen. NSW guidance says that if you're unhappy with an agent, you should end your agreement with them before signing with another agent, or both agents may charge you commission when the home sells.

NSW Government guidance also says an agency agreement must state warnings about the circumstances in which you might have to pay commission to more than one agent, and a warning if commission is payable even if the sale isn't completed. Read those warnings, and tell any new agent about agreements you've already signed.

How long does the agreement run, and how do you end it?

An agency agreement can be open-ended or for a fixed term, and NSW guidance says there is no minimum or maximum term: you and the agent negotiate it. To end the agreement you must give written notice, so check how much notice your agreement requires.

Before you sign a sole or exclusive agreement, check the end date and whether commission could still be payable after it ends, for example if a buyer who started negotiating during the term buys later.

Is there a cooling-off period on an agency agreement?

Yes. For an agency agreement to sell residential property or rural land in NSW, there is a cooling-off period that starts when you sign and ends at 5pm on the next business day or Saturday (public holidays don't count).

To cancel in that time, you give the agent a signed written notice of rescission, and the agent can't charge you fees or costs for an agreement rescinded correctly. You can waive the cooling-off period only if the agent gave you the proposed (unsigned) agreement and the approved consumer guide at least one business day before you sign, and you sign the approved waiver form.

Which type of agreement should you choose?

It depends on your situation and what you and the agent agree. Whatever you choose, NSW guidance says you can negotiate the terms, commissions, fees and expenses, and you should get legal advice if you're not sure about the terms.

Questions worth asking each agent at the appraisal (see What to ask at a property appraisal):

  • Is this an exclusive, sole or open agency agreement, and why do you recommend that type?
  • If I find a buyer myself, would I owe you commission?
  • What is the term, how much written notice do I need to give to end it, and could commission be payable after it ends?
  • What fees and expenses would I pay, and would I pay any of them if the home doesn't sell?

NSW guidance suggests talking to a few agents and asking each for a printed list of their fees, commission rates and expenses. For more on comparing agents, see How to choose a selling agent in the Hunter.

What should you check before you sign?

  • The type of agency (exclusive, sole, open/general, multiple listing or auction) is written into the agreement
  • When commission is payable, including if you or another agent finds the buyer
  • The warnings about paying more than one commission, and about any commission payable if the sale isn't completed
  • The term, the written notice needed to end it, and any commission payable after it ends
  • Any earlier agency agreement for the same home has been properly ended
  • You've been given the approved guide Agency agreements for the sale of residential property before signing
  • You receive a copy of the agreement signed by the agent within 48 hours of signing
  • The agent's licence is current (see How to check a real estate agent's licence in NSW)

FAQ

Is a sole agency the same as an exclusive agency in NSW? No. NSW guidance says that under a sole agency agreement you pay no commission if you find a buyer the agent didn't introduce, while under an exclusive agency agreement the agent may be owed commission even if you or another agent sells during the fixed term.

What is an open listing? An open listing, also called a general listing or open agency agreement, lets you list your home with a number of agents. You pay commission to the agent who finds the buyer.

Can I end up paying two agents commission? It can happen. NSW guidance says to end your agreement with one agent before signing with another, or both agents may charge you commission when the home sells.

Is there a minimum or maximum term for an agency agreement? No. NSW guidance says the term is negotiated between you and the agent, and you must give written notice to end the agreement.

Can I cancel an agency agreement after signing? Usually, during the cooling-off period, which ends at 5pm on the next business day or Saturday, unless you validly waived it. You give the agent a signed written notice of rescission.
