Cooling-off periods when selling in NSW: agency agreement vs buyer contract

NSW sellers: one-business-day cooling-off on the agency agreement, five business days for buyers after exchange, no cooling-off for sellers once contracts are exchanged.

Short answer: In NSW there are two different cooling-off rules sellers need to keep straight. On your selling agency agreement you usually get one business day (including Saturday) to cancel after you sign. On the sale contract after exchange, the buyer usually gets five business days — and once contracts are exchanged, sellers have no cooling-off period.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. Rules can change, so check the current NSW Government and NSW Fair Trading guidance, and get advice from a solicitor or conveyancer before you sign or waive anything.

This guide sits alongside Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first and What a contract for sale needs before your home is advertised in NSW.

What's the difference between the two cooling-off periods?

One is about your contract with the agent; the other is about the buyer's contract with you. The agency-agreement cooling-off is short (one business day / Saturday) and protects the seller who just signed up an agent. The sale-contract cooling-off is longer (usually five business days after exchange) and protects the buyer — not the seller.

What is the cooling-off on a selling agency agreement?

After you and the agent have both signed, NSW Government guidance says there is a cooling-off period of one business day during which you can cancel (rescind) the agreement. It starts when you sign and ends at 5pm on the next business day or Saturday. Public holidays are not included.

NSW Fair Trading (for agents) says the same timing under section 59 of the Property and Stock Agents Act 2002: the period starts when the agreement is signed and ends at 5pm on the next day that is a business day or a Saturday. Public holidays, bank holidays and Sundays are not included. Examples from the NSW consumer page: if you sign on Friday, cooling-off ends at 5pm Saturday; if you sign on Saturday, it usually ends at 5pm Monday (or Tuesday if Monday is a public holiday). The length can also be extended by a term in the agreement or by a written statement from the agent before the usual period ends.

How do you cancel an agency agreement during cooling-off?

Deliver a written notice of rescission to the agent. NSW guidance says the notice must be addressed to the agent (use the name in the agreement), state that you are rescinding the agreement, and be signed by you and any other person named as a principal, or by your solicitor/s.

You can hand it to the agent, leave it at the agent's office or the address in the agreement, or email it (NSW Fair Trading also lists fax). Keep a copy. If the agreement is correctly rescinded, the agent cannot charge fees or costs, and any money already paid must be refunded in full.

Can you waive the agency-agreement cooling-off?

Yes, but only if the conditions are met. NSW consumer guidance says you can waive by signing a separate waiver form when you sign the agreement, and only if the agent gave you a copy of the proposed (unsigned) agency agreement and a copy of the waiver form at least one business day before you signed. Legal advice is recommended.

NSW Fair Trading's professionals page adds precision for residential sales: the agent must give you the unsigned agreement and the approved guide "Agency agreements for the sale of residential property" at least one business day before you sign, and you must sign the NSW Fair Trading approved waiver form before signing the agency agreement. When those documents are given, Saturday is not recognised as a business day. Seek advice before you waive.

What about the cooling-off on the buyer's sale contract?

After contracts are exchanged on a residential property in NSW, the buyer usually has a five business day cooling-off period. NSW guidance says it starts on exchange and ends at 5pm on the fifth business day after the day of exchange. Example: exchange at 10am Tuesday → ends at 5pm Tuesday the following week. Off-the-plan purchases usually get 10 business days.

If the buyer withdraws during cooling-off, they pay the vendor 0.25% of the purchase price ($250 per $100,000). Buyers can waive cooling-off with a '66W certificate', or reduce or extend it by written agreement with the vendor — they should seek advice before waiving.

Do sellers get a cooling-off after exchange?

No. NSW Fair Trading says there is no cooling-off period for sellers: once contracts have been exchanged, sellers must uphold the agreement. That is why your solicitor or conveyancer should review the contract carefully before exchange. See also Auction vs private treaty in the Hunter and Selling costs and timeline in the Hunter.

Does auction change cooling-off?

Yes for buyers. NSW guidance says there is no cooling-off if you buy at auction, or if you exchange contracts on the same day as the auction after the property is passed in. The agency-agreement cooling-off still applies when you first sign up your selling agent, including for auction agency agreements — that is a separate contract between you and the agent.

What should sellers watch for before signing either document?

Read the agency agreement carefully (fees, estimated selling price, authority to exchange or change the sale contract) and know your one-business-day cooling-off before you waive it — see Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW and Sole agency vs open listing in NSW. Make sure the contract for sale is ready before advertising — see What a contract for sale needs before your home is advertised in NSW. Once you exchange, remember you have no cooling-off as the seller. If you haven't chosen an agent yet, see How to choose a selling agent in the Hunter.

FAQ

What's the cooling-off period on a NSW selling agency agreement? Usually one business day (or Saturday): it starts when you sign and ends at 5pm on the next business day or Saturday. Public holidays, bank holidays and Sundays are not included.

How do I cancel an agency agreement during cooling-off in NSW? Give the agent a written notice of rescission addressed to them, stating you are rescinding, signed by you (and any other principals) or your solicitor. You can hand deliver, leave it at the agent's office or address in the agreement, or email it. Keep a copy.

Do sellers get a cooling-off period after exchange in NSW? No. NSW Fair Trading says once contracts are exchanged, sellers must uphold the agreement. The usual five business day cooling-off after exchange is for buyers.

Is there cooling-off if the property sells at auction in NSW? Buyers generally have no cooling-off at auction, or if they exchange on the same day after the property is passed in. Your separate agency-agreement cooling-off with the selling agent still applies when you first sign that agreement.

Can the agency-agreement cooling-off be waived? Only if the agent gave you the unsigned agreement (and, for residential sales, the approved agency-agreements guide) at least one business day before you signed, and you sign the NSW Fair Trading approved waiver form before signing the agreement. Saturday is not a business day for that lead time. Seek legal advice before waiving.
