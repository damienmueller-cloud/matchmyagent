Interviewing agents in Newcastle: a one-page comparison checklist (printable)

Compare real estate agents in Newcastle with a printable one-page checklist: licence check, price estimate evidence, fees and commission, and the agreement type.

Short answer: To compare real estate agents in Newcastle, interview at least three licensed agents, ask each one the same questions, and write their answers side by side on one page. NSW Government guidance suggests speaking with at least 3 agents and comparing their licence, fees, local knowledge, commission and how they'll promote your home.

General information only, not legal or financial advice. Rules can change, so check the current NSW Fair Trading guidance or ask a solicitor or conveyancer about your situation.

This guide turns How to choose a selling agent in the Hunter and What to ask at a property appraisal into one sheet you can print and take to each interview. Jump to the printable checklist.

How do you compare real estate agents in Newcastle?

Ask every agent the same questions and record the answers in the same place, so you're comparing like with like rather than whoever gave the best pitch. NSW Government guidance suggests you speak with at least 3 agents before choosing.

The steps NSW guidance lists for choosing an agent are: check they have a current licence, ask about fees and charges, check for professional indemnity insurance, ask about any compliance issues, assess their knowledge of your local area, get and compare quotes and commission rates, find out how they'll promote your property, then sign an agency agreement. The checklist below follows that order.

What should you check before the interview?

Check each agent's licence and ask for their fees in writing before you meet. Both are quick and give you a fair starting point.

  • Licence: look the agent up on Verify NSW or call 13 32 20. The licence check shows the licence category, expiry and any disciplinary action. See How to check a real estate agent's licence in NSW.
  • Fees: NSW guidance suggests asking each agent for a printed list of their fees, commission rates and the expenses (for example, advertising) they charge.
  • Names from neighbours: the NSW Fair Trading approved guide suggests, if possible, getting the names of one or two agents from other home owners in your area who have recently sold.

What questions should you ask each agent?

Ask about local knowledge, the price estimate and its evidence, how they'd sell and promote your home, who does the work, and what it costs. Keep the questions identical for every agent.

The printable checklist has a row for each. For more detail on the pricing and campaign questions, see What to ask at a property appraisal. The NSW approved guide also suggests asking whether the agent follows a code of ethics.

How do you compare different price estimates fairly?

Compare the evidence, not just the number. In NSW, an agent must include an estimated selling price in the agency agreement, make sure it's reasonable, and give you evidence supporting it, such as recent comparable sales.

If the estimate is a range, NSW rules limit how wide the range can be. NSW guidance says that if different agents give you different estimates, each must provide evidence, and you should be cautious of much higher estimates, as they may not be reasonable. More changes to the underquoting laws, including a Statement of Information with comparable sales, are expected to start on a date to be announced, which NSW Fair Trading says is currently expected to be towards the end of 2026. See Estimated selling price and underquoting rules in NSW.

Is real estate commission negotiable in NSW?

Yes. The NSW Fair Trading approved guide says the amounts agents charge are not set by law, and you can negotiate commissions, fees and other expenses.

NSW guidance also warns that where commission changes based on price, you may end up paying significantly more than under a flat fee, so do your calculations and know what you'd owe at the end of the sale. Ask whether any costs, such as advertising, are payable upfront or even if the home doesn't sell.

What should you ask about the agency agreement?

Ask what type of agreement the agent proposes, how long it runs, and how you'd end it. These terms affect who you pay commission to and when.

An agency agreement must state the services, fees or commission, when the agent is entitled to payment, the agent's estimated selling price, and warnings about when you might pay commission to more than one agent. There's no minimum or maximum term, and you end it by written notice. See Sole agency vs open listing in NSW and Signing a selling agency agreement in NSW: what to check first.

What are the warning signs in an agent interview?

Be wary of pressure to sign straight away, an estimate much higher than the others with no evidence, and vague answers about fees. NSW guidance says not to be pressured by the agent, and to re-negotiate or find another agent if you're not satisfied.

Other things worth noting on the sheet: a licence that isn't current, reluctance to put fees in writing, or no clear answer about who will run your campaign.

What happens after you choose an agent?

You sign a written agency agreement before the agent can market your home. For residential property, the agent must give you the approved guide Agency agreements for the sale of residential property before you sign, and serve you a copy of the agreement they've signed within 48 hours.

There's then a cooling-off period that ends at 5pm on the next business day or Saturday (public holidays don't count), unless you validly waived it. To cancel in that time, give the agent a signed written notice of rescission.

FAQ

How many agents should I interview? NSW Government guidance suggests speaking with at least 3 agents before choosing one.

Can I ask an agent for their fees in writing? Yes. NSW guidance suggests asking each agent for a printed list of their fees, commission rates and expenses, such as advertising, before signing an agreement.

Is commission negotiable in NSW? Yes. The NSW Fair Trading approved guide says the amounts agents charge are not set by law and you can negotiate commissions, fees and other expenses.

Why do agents give different price estimates? Estimates can vary, but NSW guidance says each agent must give evidence supporting their estimate, and you should be cautious of much higher estimates, as they may not be reasonable.

How do I check an agent is licensed in NSW? Use the Verify NSW licence check or call 13 32 20. It shows the licence category, expiry and any disciplinary action.
